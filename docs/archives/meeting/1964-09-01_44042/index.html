Austin Settles Electrical Price-Fixing Lawsuits

Tuesday, September 1, 1964 special called
  • Austin City Council voted to accept settlements totaling over $639,000 from Westinghouse and General Electric, concluding the city's participation in national antitrust lawsuits against electrical equipment manufacturers for price-fixing.
  • The decision followed strong recommendations from city attorneys, who had initially aimed for a higher settlement. Their change of heart was due to federal judges presiding over the cases, who urged acceptance, citing widespread similar settlements nationwide and indicating an unfavorable climate if the cases went to trial.
  • The vote was divided, with some council members expressing concern over the settlement amounts, perceived judicial pressure, and the potential for greater compensation (including triple damages) if the cases proceeded to trial.
  • Despite these settlements, the City of Austin decided to continue litigation against other manufacturers whose offers were deemed inadequate.
