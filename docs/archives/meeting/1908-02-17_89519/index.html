Austin's 1908 Infrastructure Plans and Budget

Monday, February 17, 1908 regular

• Street and Infrastructure Improvements: The council approved funding for Congress Avenue paving south of Third Street ($2,500), authorized street work near Wooldridge School, and pushed the railroad company to raise its tracks and remove street obstructions within 10 days or face franchise forfeiture.

• New Garbage Disposal System: The council allocated $1,000 in the annual budget to build a modern garbage crematory to handle the city's daily waste—a significant public health infrastructure investment for the era.

• Fire Safety Upgrades: Multiple fire hydrants were approved for placement on 25th/27th streets and at the corner of East 12th and Alamo Street, with orders for a complete inspection of all existing hydrants.

• Mayor Vetoed Outside Legal Contract: Mayor F.M. Maddox blocked an ordinance to hire outside counsel for tax collection, arguing the city attorney and assessor were already handling the work effectively; the council failed to override his veto.

• Budget and Personnel Decisions: The council adopted the 1908 budget (with amendments), approved salary ordinances for city employees and police, and confirmed two new plumbing examiners.

Austin, Texas, February 17, 09 To the Honorable, the City Council of the City of Austin. Gentlemen: Mayors Veto. I respectfully herewith to return to you, without my approval, Contract work your ordinance, entitled, " An ordinance, authorizing and instructing Kyrie Trasher the Mayor of the City of Austin to employ Kyrie Trasher as agent and attorney for the City of Austin for the purpose of causing the assess-ment and collection of such taxes due said City." Upon investigation of this matter I find that the Assessor and Collector of taxes, placed in the hands of the City Attorney, Wm Hart, the assessment against the Corporations provided for in your ordinance, and that the City Attorney has made and is making at this time, efforts to collect same; and that he has collected from some of these corporations taxes due the City of Austin up to the time they were placed in the hands of a Receiver, and that since the placing of same in the hands of a Receiver, he was waiting the closing of the Receivership to collect same. I find that the matter was having proper and prompt attention by both the Assessor and Collector of Taxes of the City of Austin and the City Attorney, and that the interests of the City are being well and carefully guarded. In justice to Mr Trasher I wish to state that he was honest and sincere in what he thought to be the facts in the case. For these reasons I do not think it proper to employ or pay outside counsel for work that has been and is now being done by duly constituted Officers of the City. Respectfully submitted., F M Maddox, Mayor.
