Austin Fights COVID: Surge, Shots, and Schools

Tuesday, January 12, 2021 Austin City Council Special Called Meeting

  • Critical COVID-19 Surge:

    Austin faces an uncontrolled surge, with hospitals nearing capacity (ICUs at 180 patients for three days, 100% increase in a month). Residents are urged to stay home, limit gatherings, and avoid non-essential travel under Stage 5 recommendations.

  • Vaccine Rollout Begins:

    Austin Public Health (APH) received 12,000 vaccine doses, prioritizing healthcare workers and vulnerable populations (age 65+). A public online portal for vaccine appointments launches January 13th, aiming for equitable distribution in hardest-hit communities.

  • School Concerns Rise:

    High positivity rates are seen in school-age children (up to 27% in middle school), with clusters tied to extracurriculars and shared transportation. Virtual learning is strongly recommended to protect students and educators.

  • New Treatment and Care Sites:

    A state-supported infusion center is now treating COVID-19 patients with monoclonal antibodies to reduce hospitalizations. An alternate care site at the Convention Center has also opened to decompress hospitals, ready to expand if needed.

  • Regional Collaboration Needed:

    Efforts are underway to create a regional vaccine distribution and tracking system with neighboring counties to streamline access, but widespread availability is not expected until around April.
