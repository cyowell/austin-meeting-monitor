Fair Housing, Energy Rules, Historic Site Fails

Thursday, June 2, 1977 regular

  • Major Fair Housing Debate:

    A lengthy public hearing was held on a proposed Fair Housing Ordinance, sparking extensive debate over issues of discrimination (including creed, occupation, family status, and disability), local enforcement, and potential overlaps with federal law. Final action was postponed for further review.

  • City Board Appointment Reform Sought:

    Concerns were raised regarding unequal representation on city boards and commissions, with residents proposing new procedures for greater transparency and equitable selection of appointees from all Austin neighborhoods.

  • New Energy Conservation Rules & Delays:

    An ordinance was passed requiring electricity sub-metering in apartments and mobile home parks to promote energy savings. Separately, the implementation of stricter Energy Efficiency Ratio (EER) standards for air conditioning units was delayed until 1978 due to equipment availability.

  • Historic Zoning Defeated:

    A proposal to designate a property at 700 West 6th Street as historic failed, despite a recommendation from the Landmark Commission, after the owner successfully argued economic hardship and the measure did not secure the required supermajority vote.
