Austin Street Improvements and Railroad Franchise Approved

Monday, September 21, 1903 regular

• Mayor Vetoes Street Funding: Mayor R.E. White rejected a $530 appropriation for 12th Street improvements because it didn't meet the required vote threshold (7 votes weren't enough for a $500+ expense). The Council upheld his veto.

• Multiple Infrastructure Projects Approved: The city approved spending on various street improvements, including $250 for Canal Street grading, $75 for a footbridge on West 6th Street, and $500 for extra street labor and teams.

• Railroad Gets Permission to Expand: The Houston Texas Central Railroad received approval to build a spur track extension across Meches Street in downtown Austin.

• Memorial Fountain Planned: A committee was appointed to work with Mrs. O.P. Dylehart on plans for a monumental water fountain to honor her late husband.

• Routine Business Conducted: The Council denied salary increase requests, approved payment for park improvements, and ordered the removal of old dummy train tracks from East 4th Street.

To the Hon City Council. I return herewith an ordinance entitled "An Ordinance appropriating the sum of $530.00 for the purpose of paying for extra street labor teams and laborers to 12th Street between Rio Grande and Placed Streets, passed Sept 8, 1903, without my approval. My reason for vetoing the above styled ordinance is the fact that the vote on the final passage of said ordinance was seven for and six against the passage of the ordinance. Section 13 of the Charter of the City of Austin provides "that a majority of the members of the whole Council shall be necessary to pass an ordinance for any purpose appropriating the sum of five hundred dollars or upwards." The whole Council being composed of fourteen members,- under the foregoing Charter provision, it requires more than seven votes to legally pass the said ordinance. Sep 9/03 R.E.
