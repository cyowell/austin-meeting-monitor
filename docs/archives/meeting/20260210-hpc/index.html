Austin Housing Future: Zoning & Affordability

Tuesday, February 10, 2026 Housing and Planning Committee Regular Meeting

Here's a summary of what the Austin City Council Housing and Planning Committee ACTUALLY DID at their February 10, 2026 meeting:

  • Approved Previous Meeting Minutes:

    The committee started by formally approving the minutes from its January 27th meeting.

  • Reviewed Land Development Code Updates and Delays:

    City staff reported that City Council recently adopted an amendment related to parkland drainage easements. However, several other planned amendments to the Land Development Code are now on hold, awaiting further coordination with city leadership. A study exploring new zoning options to support mixed-use and "missing middle" housing was also released.

  • Grappled with Austin's Affordable Housing Crisis:

    The committee received a detailed briefing on the severe shortage of affordable housing, particularly for residents earning less than 60% of the median family income. Discussion focused on how a new state law (SB 840) has reduced the effectiveness of existing density bonus programs. Council members explored shifting the city's focus to create more housing for the lowest income levels (30-50% MFI) and considering "fee-in-lieu" payments from developers to fund these crucial efforts.

  • Updated on Long-Range City Planning Initiatives:

    Staff provided an overview of various ongoing and upcoming projects, including updates to the "Imagine Austin" comprehensive plan, specific district plans for Central City and Northeast Austin, and a modernization of the "Great Streets" program for downtown. As part of the Northeast District plan, the city is also beginning a five-month pilot project focused on improving food access.

  • Expressed Frustration Over Housing Delivery and Funding:

    Council members voiced concerns about the difficulty of translating housing plans into actual completed units on the ground, especially in environmentally sensitive areas. They emphasized the need for more innovative financing strategies and partnerships to preserve existing affordable housing and build new deeply affordable units, acknowledging state limitations on tools like local minimum wage increases.
