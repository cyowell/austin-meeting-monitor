Austin's 1950: Buses, Parks, and Hidden Gold

Thursday, May 18, 1950 regular

  • Public Transit Revamp:

    The city council approved a comprehensive overhaul of bus services, introducing a $1.25 unlimited weekly pass, a 10-cent off-peak "shopper's special" (9 am - 3:30 pm), and several route changes to improve efficiency and reduce congestion.

  • New Taxi and Alcohol Rules:

    Major new ordinances were introduced for city-wide regulation of taxicab services (including franchises, meters, and fares) and the sale, storage, and handling of alcoholic beverages.

  • Community Projects & Contested Zoning:

    Funds were approved for a new public softball stadium, and a large 20-acre tract was designated for a future park. However, a controversial zoning change on Hancock Drive, permitting commercial use, passed despite strong opposition from residents, churches, and schools during a two-hour public hearing.

  • Unique Treasure Hunting Deal:

    In an unusual move, the city authorized a contract allowing an individual to search for buried treasure on city property, with the city set to receive 25% of any discoveries.

  • Major Street Renaming Initiative:

    Several significant city roads, including parts of Georgetown Road and Fredricksburg Road, were officially renamed, leading to a major expansion of Lamar Boulevard and the introduction of new street names like West Guadalupe Street.
