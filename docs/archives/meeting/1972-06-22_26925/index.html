Transforming Austin: Taxes, Land, and Lifestyle

Thursday, June 22, 1972 regular

  • Major Property Tax Overhaul:

    The Council approved significant reforms to Austin's property tax system, including eliminating the "quadrant system" for appraisals, pausing city-wide re-evaluations for 1973, and mandating separate City and School tax bills starting in 1974 to improve transparency for residents.

  • Aggressive City Expansion & Infrastructure:

    Thousands of acres were approved for annexation proceedings, signaling rapid city growth. Key infrastructure projects were advanced, such as acquiring land for Mo-Pac Boulevard and expanding the Walnut Creek Treatment Plant, alongside the approval of a large new Municipal Utility District.

  • New Community Amenities & Recreation:

    Plans were set in motion for a new recreation center in South Austin, and the city manager was directed to explore developing 158 acres near the Walnut Creek plant into a golf course or other recreational facilities.

  • Addressing Social & Urban Challenges:

    A public hearing was scheduled to potentially reconsider the city's primary anti-poverty agency. Additionally, a new ordinance was passed to strengthen dog bite regulations, and citizen concerns about neighborhood decline in the West University area prompted a commitment to a comprehensive study of zoning and city services.
