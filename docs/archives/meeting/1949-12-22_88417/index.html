Compost, Paving, Parks, and Power

Thursday, December 22, 1949 regular

  • Pioneering Waste Program:

    The City entered a 5-year contract with St. Edward's University to convert city refuse and garbage into compost for fertilizer, a notably early "green" initiative that also helped dispose of waste.

  • Major Infrastructure Investments:

    Plans advanced for paving 27 city streets (including Travis Heights Boulevard), with a public hearing scheduled for January 1950 to discuss property owner assessments. A significant contract was also awarded for new power plant piping.

  • Expanding Commercial Development:

    Several key zoning changes were approved, transforming residential areas into commercial zones. Notable rezonings included parts of Lamar Boulevard and an area for a planned "Kiddy Amusement Park."

  • New Park and Asset Management:

    The City officially named its newly acquired Country Club site "Hancock Park," authorized the purchase of its furnishings, and began negotiations for the sale of numerous parcels of excess city-owned property.
