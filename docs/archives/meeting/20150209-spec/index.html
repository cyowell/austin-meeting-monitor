Austin's Water Future: Scarcity, Strategy, & Costs

Monday, February 9, 2015 Austin City Council Special Called Meeting
  • Austin is in its worst drought on record, with primary water lakes at a critical 35% capacity, prompting plans for potential Stage 3/4 water restrictions and surcharges if levels continue to drop.
  • Despite successful resident conservation, the city's water utility faces financial challenges, including a negative bond rating outlook and 66% of customers being billed below the cost of service.
  • A long-term "Integrated Water Resource Plan" (50-100 year horizon) is urgently needed to diversify Austin's 100% reliance on the Colorado River, exploring new sources like groundwater, treated wastewater reuse, and storm water capture.
  • Future infrastructure plans include multi-purpose "green infrastructure" (for water, flood control, energy, and green space) and encouraging dual-plumbing for non-potable uses in new developments.
  • The meeting also addressed ongoing legal disputes over wholesale water rates and concerns that development around Decker Lake could compromise its potential use as a vital water storage resource.
