Austin: Annexations, Airport, and Cold War Prep

Thursday, October 24, 1963 regular

Here's a summary of the Austin City Council agenda:

  • Significant City Expansion & Infrastructure:

    The council approved the annexation of over 165 acres across several areas, including future sites for schools and residential developments. They also greenlit major infrastructure projects, such as new underground telephone conduits and a jet fuel pipeline.

  • Airport Development with Civil Rights Commitment:

    Austin accepted a $47,000 federal grant for a parking lot at Robert Mueller Municipal Airport, with the notable condition of a non-discrimination clause prohibiting bias based on race, color, creed, or national origin in airport facilities.

  • Traffic Safety & Cold War Preparedness:

    New 20 mph speed limits were established around schools during operating hours. Separately, the City Hall basement was officially approved and designated as a fallout shelter for emergency operations, reflecting Cold War era concerns.

  • Enhanced Employee Benefits & Public Input:

    City employees received significantly increased hospitalization benefits at no additional premium. The council also heard public suggestions for reforming traffic police practices and denying a commercial banner request to preserve public space.
