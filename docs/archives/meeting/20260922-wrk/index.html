Austin's 911 Call Center Future Sparks Debate

  • 911 Call Center's Future Sparks Debate:

    The Council held an extensive work session discussing the future of Austin's 911 call-taking services, following a new feasibility study. No formal votes were taken at this briefing.

  • Consultant Recommends Modernization, Not Separation:

    Mission Critical Partners (MCP) advised against creating a new, independent 911 department. They cited state-level legislative and contractual challenges, alongside a significantly higher estimated cost (over $11 million for setup) compared to proposed enhancements within the current structure ($1.7 million).

  • Key Enhancements Proposed for 911:

    MCP recommended strengthening the existing 911 system within the Austin Police Department (APD). This includes establishing civilian leadership for 911 call takers (who would report to the Chief of Police), creating a dedicated 4-person Quality Assurance unit, and upgrading technology to improve call outcomes.

  • Public Opinion Divided:

    Residents voiced strong, conflicting views on the 911 center's future. Some advocated for an independent center to improve call routing for mental health crises and address community mistrust. Others supported the consultant's plan, highlighting recent improvements in 911 response times and existing mental health support.

  • Social Service Contracts Discussed:

    The Council heard public comments requesting a transparent new process for awarding social service contracts, with calls for meaningful input from non-profits and broader inclusion of related service agreements.
