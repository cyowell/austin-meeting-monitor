Gas Rates, New Sign Rules, Taxi Fares, City Upgrades

Thursday, January 15, 1976 regular

  • Gas Franchise Under Fire

    A major public hearing saw strong opposition to Southern Union Gas Company's proposed changes, including new definitions for gas, altered service extension policies, and increased fees. Decisions were postponed due to public and City Attorney concerns.

  • New Sign Regulations Spark Debate

    A comprehensive ordinance aimed at updating sign rules, particularly impacting real estate and commercial signage, faced scrutiny from businesses. The proposed changes, including amortization for existing signs, led to a delay in final adoption.

  • Taxi Fares Climb, Service Expected to Improve

    Taxi rates were increased (e.g., 70 cents for the first 1/4 mile), and permit fees rose, while charges for extra passengers were eliminated. This move aimed to stabilize the struggling taxi industry.

  • Investing in City Infrastructure & Services

    Over $1 million was approved for 18 new transit buses, along with significant appropriations for Brackenridge Hospital upgrades and parking. The city also pushed forward with essential utility projects and explored sites for a new Senior Citizen Center.
