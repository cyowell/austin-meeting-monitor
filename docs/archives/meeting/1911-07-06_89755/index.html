Austin Dam Project, Alley & Fence Rules

Thursday, July 6, 1911 regular

  • Austin Dam Rebuilding Project Approved:

    The City approved a major contract with William D. Johnson to rebuild the Austin Dam. The agreement includes specifications for new turbines and generators, a 25-year financial commitment ($100,000 upon completion plus $64,800 annually), and the city operating the plant, with the final ordinance subject to citizen approval.

  • Private Alley Permanently Closed:

    An 18-foot alley-way in the Gypsy Grove Addition was permanently closed and vacated by resolution, following a petition from all affected property owners who stated the alley had never been used and was already privately enclosed.

  • Shooting Gallery Permit Granted:

    H.C. Edgar received a one-year permit to operate a shooting gallery at Riverside Park.

  • Barb Wire Fence Ban Progresses:

    An ordinance prohibiting the construction and maintenance of barb wire fences moved closer to becoming law, passing its second reading.

The Mayor laid before the Council the resolution heretofore offered by Councilman Lockridge in regard to propositions for rebuilding the dam, which was read. Mayor Wooldridge offered the following as a substitute for the resolution vis: Be it resolved by the City Council of the City of Austin: That the City of Austin hereby accepts the proposition of William D Johnson to rebuild the Austin dam, in accordance with the proposal, plans and specifications submitted by him to this City on the last day of June 1911, with such changed therein for the benefit of this City as mayhave been submitted to the said William D Johnson by members of this Council, and agreed to by his authorized agent, Mr Frank S Taylor. The principal features of said Johnson's proposition, with changes proposed to said Johnson and accepted by him, and adopted by this resolution, are the following: (1) The present proposition to stand as it is, except that the reservoir shall be built with the same capacity on site to be selected by the City. (2) That there shall be three turbines of 2,400 H P each. (3) Three generators of 2,250 capacity maximum, with normal rating of not less than 2,000 H P each (any two units to deliver to City's power house 4,000 H P. Net ). (4) The City to operate and maintain the plant at the dam, the trans-mission line and the pump station at City steam power plant. (5) Johnson to guarantee 600.000 H P hrs per month whenever the water in the lake is less than one foot below crest of the dam, the City to have all excess power, and the said Johnson to be charged for City's shortage of his guaranteed power at the rate of .0135 cents per H P hour. (6) Johnson to convey all patent rights for appliances used on his contract to the City in so far as they relate to that particular work (7) The City shall pay to Johnson the sum of Sixty-four thousand, eight hundred dollars ($ 64,800.00 ) annually, in semi-annual payments in addition to the Hundred thousand dollars ($ 100,000.00) when the dam is completed ,except the terms shall be for twenty five years, instead of twenty six years as in the present proposition. (8).All forfeitures for continuation of work ,particularly in dam or appliances, to remain as in the original proposition. (9) The twenty four inch water main to be carried on upper side of dam, if reservoir is located on West side of River, in which case the track on the dam and the two cars mentioned in the specification are to be provided by the said William D Johnson. Be it further resolved, that the City Attorney be instructed to confer with the said William D Johnson, or his agent, as to the drawing proper ordinance containing these conditions, and when so drawn it shall be presented to the Citizens for their approval. --- Page break --- 763 The substitute was adopted by the following vote : Yeas Mayor Woodridge, Councilmen Bartholomew, Hart & Powell 4 Nays Councilman Lockridge 1 xxxxx The Council then recessed subject to call of the Mayor.
