Austin's Future: Planning, Parks, and Prices

Thursday, February 27, 1964 regular

Here's a look at key decisions from Austin's City Council meeting on February 27, 1964:

  • Urban Renewal & Federal Planning:

    The council approved a federally-backed Community Renewal Program, dedicating funds to a comprehensive study of urban blight, housing, and socio-economic factors across the city, despite debates over the $38,000 local contribution.

  • Recreation Costs Rise:

    Fees for public services like city golf courses, swimming pools (including a new charge for young children at paid pools), camping, and tennis courts were significantly increased, sparking discussion about public access.

  • Zoning Reflects Community Values:

    Council denied a commercial zoning request for a proposed drive-in near a church, overriding a planning commission recommendation due to community opposition concerning potential alcohol sales. Several other zoning changes were approved, with some controversial requests deferred for further review.

  • Infrastructure & Growth Investments:

    The city moved forward with land acquisitions for a new park in the Kealing Project urban renewal area and authorized purchases for major street widenings (including the 29th Street extension) and airport expansion to support ongoing development.
