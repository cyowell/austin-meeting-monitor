Austin: New X-Rays, Jobs, Roads, Civil Rights

Thursday, September 28, 1933 regular

  • Addressed Civil Rights Concerns:

    The City Attorney was tasked with investigating alleged discrimination by insurance companies against Black taxicab operators seeking required bonds.

  • Launched Extensive Work Relief Programs:

    Multiple public works projects (e.g., street, alley, drainage, and park improvements) were approved, securing state and federal aid to cover labor costs during the Great Depression.

  • Secured Federal Funding for Highway Development:

    The city agreed to federal conditions, including a minimum 20 mph speed limit, to gain aid for constructing State Highway 71 on East First Street.

  • Invested in Public Health and Responded to Feedback:

    Funds were appropriated for new X-ray equipment, and several street names (e.g., Loomis) were reverted to previous numerical designations (e.g., East 19.5 Street) following resident requests.
