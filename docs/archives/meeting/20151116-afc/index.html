Austin Addresses Housing Data and Lobbying

Monday, November 16, 2015 Audit and Finance Committee Regular Meeting

  • Affordable Housing Audit Finds Major Flaws:

    An audit revealed Austin's affordable housing programs overstated success by thousands of units, lacked a clear strategy for long-term, deeper affordability, and had inconsistent monitoring of existing affordable homes.

  • Developer Incentives Questioned:

    City officials acknowledged that developer incentives (like Smart Housing) often aren't designed for the deepest or longest-term affordability, prompting calls for a serious policy reevaluation of how these programs contribute to housing goals.

  • New Lobbying Rules Proposed:

    A draft resolution introduced amendments to Austin's lobbying ordinance, including higher thresholds for registration, clearer definitions of what constitutes lobbying, and a shift from criminal to civil penalties.

  • Transparency vs. Administrative Burden:

    A controversial proposal would require any individual communicating with city staff on municipal matters to sign in, aiming for broad transparency, but raising concerns from the City Clerk about immense administrative challenges.
