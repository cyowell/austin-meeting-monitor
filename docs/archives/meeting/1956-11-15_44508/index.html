Austin Debates Museum, Approves Airport Deal

Thursday, November 15, 1956 regular

  • O. Henry Museum Site Sparks Protest:

    Residents voiced strong opposition to the city's plan to move the O. Henry Museum and Honeymoon House to a new Lamar Boulevard location, a decision the city council ultimately upheld.

  • Airline Agreements Secured:

    The city renewed three-year agreements with major carriers Braniff, Continental, and Trans-Texas Air Lines, incorporating updated landing fees for the municipal airport.

  • River Sand Dredging Approved:

    A contract was authorized for a company to excavate sand and gravel from the river between the Railroad and Lamar Bridges, with the city emphasizing close supervision for dam protection and proper fill.

  • Urban Expansion and Infrastructure:

    The council approved several zoning changes for retail and office development, facilitated new gas and telephone utility lines, and funded the paving of Berkman Drive, sharing costs with a developer.

  • Further City Limit Growth:

    The city annexed an additional 0.704-acre territory and initiated the process to bring another adjacent lot into city limits.
