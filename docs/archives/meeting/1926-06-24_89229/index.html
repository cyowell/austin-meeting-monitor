Powering Austin: Dam Deal, Driver Refunds, Electric Safety

Thursday, June 24, 1926 regular

Here are the key takeaways from the June 24, 1926 Austin City Council agenda:

  • Major Power and Dam Proposal:

    The City Council considered a significant offer from Texas Power & Light Company to acquire, rehabilitate, and complete the Austin Dam and its hydroelectric station. This deal, intended to connect the city to a broader power grid and supply electricity, was made contingent on approval by Austin voters in a future election.

  • Refunds for Illegal Driver Fees:

    The city moved to rectify past wrongs by authorizing over $1,500 (plus interest) in refunds to "service car operators" (early taxi or jitney drivers). These fees had been collected for years under ordinances later deemed void and illegal.

  • New Electrical Safety Code:

    A comprehensive ordinance was enacted to regulate electrical work, including licensing and bonding for "Master Electricians," mandating permits for construction, adopting the "National Electrical Code," and establishing fees and penalties to ensure public safety.

  • Land Ownership Clarification:

    The city formally relinquished its claim to a former street and part of a park from an 1872 subdivision. These areas had never been used publicly since 1873 and were officially quitclaimed to the Texas Training School for Defectives, which had occupied the land for decades.
