Austin: Federal Funds, Dam Growth, Bird Sanctuary

Thursday, March 16, 1939 regular

Here's a summary of the March 16, 1939 Austin City Council agenda:

  • Major Federal Investment:

    Austin secured substantial Public Works Administration (PWA) grants, including up to $123,750 for waterworks improvements and a new service station. The Council also approved significant PWA-funded contracts for school construction and renovations across multiple campuses, and set supplementary wage scales for labor on these federal projects.

  • Push for Dam Expansion:

    The Mayor was authorized to lobby federal representatives to increase the height of Marshall Ford Dam by at least 74 feet. This was a critical flood control measure aimed at protecting low-lying areas of South Austin and the city’s power plant.

  • New Bird Sanctuary & Wildlife Week:

    A public hearing was scheduled for a proposal to create a bird sanctuary along the shores of Lake Austin, reflecting an early interest in environmental conservation. The Mayor also officially proclaimed "National Wild Life Restoration Week."

  • City Growth and Development:

    Key decisions included advancing a plan to expand city limits, approving a residential zoning change in the Hyde Park neighborhood, and scheduling a public hearing for an industrial zoning request to permit a new lumber yard.
