Austin: Liquor, Urban Renewal Homes, Poverty Fight

Thursday, May 20, 1965 regular

  • Zoning Hot Debates:

    City officials heard impassioned arguments regarding a proposed liquor store in Windsor Park near schools and churches, ultimately deferring a decision amidst strong community opposition. Other retail zoning changes also sparked debate, including a Safeway expansion delayed by resident complaints over notification.

  • Anti-Poverty Initiative:

    A major "Community Action Program" was approved, targeting poverty through new federal "Economic Opportunity" funds. This plan includes early childhood education (day care, kindergartens), family planning and dental clinics, and a summer youth employment program.

  • Urban Renewal Houses Sold Cheap:

    Seven dilapidated houses, acquired through urban renewal efforts, were sold off for very low prices (some as little as $7.51) as the city moved to clear uninhabitable structures and avoid demolition costs.

  • Infrastructure and Property Deals:

    The council made various property decisions, including vacating sections of streets and alleys, releasing old utility easements, dedicating land for road widening (Manchaca Road), and approving a unique land trade for street improvement on Reinli Street.
