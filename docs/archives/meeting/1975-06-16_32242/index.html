Austin Community Schools Unveiled

Monday, June 16, 1975 special called
  • The Austin City Council and School Board met to explore a "Community School" concept, proposing to open school facilities for broader public education, recreation, and services after traditional school hours.
  • A Community Education Task Force recommended establishing six pilot community education centers, building on successful models already operating at Becker and Cook schools.
  • These centers would offer a range of programs, from job training and services to recreation and crime reduction initiatives, aiming to meet diverse neighborhood needs and strengthen community ties.
  • The funding model proposed a combination of self-supporting classes, an estimated $300,000 in administrative costs for the pilot year (partially tax-funded), and leveraging federal and Austin Community College resources.
  • Both the Council and School Board expressed strong support for the initiative as an effective use of tax dollars and a way to enhance city-school-community collaboration, though no final decisions were made at this work session.
