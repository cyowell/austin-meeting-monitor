Austin Police: Crime, Staffing, Growth Needs

Wednesday, February 15, 2017 Austin City Council Budget Work Session

  • Austin Police Department (APD) Budget and Crime Trends:

    APD presented its $401 million budget, noting an 8% increase in violent crime in 2016, driven by aggravated assaults and robberies. Rapid population growth continues to strain resources, leading to increased service demands and traffic congestion.

  • Addressing Staffing Shortages:

    APD reported 117 officer vacancies and is currently 77 officers short of recommendations from a 2015 study. Efforts include online recruitment tools and two cadet classes, with an aim to address major vacancies by year-end, along with a recognized need for more civilian support staff.

  • Critical Infrastructure and Technology Investments:

    Key capital projects include the urgent need for a new northwest police substation to reduce officer travel times and address overcrowding, plus potential expansion of the shared public safety training academy. The department also seeks mobile data terminals for efficiency and anticipates a significant rise in open records requests once body-worn cameras are implemented.

  • Special Event Funding and Community Engagement:

    Discussions included the financial burden of policing numerous city events, with questions raised about cost recovery from large festivals that currently receive waivers. APD also detailed progress on its community policing initiatives, focusing on developing new metrics and enhancing public engagement.
