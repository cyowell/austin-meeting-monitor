Housing Rules Tighten, Lake Drawdown Sparks Outcry

Here's what Austin City leaders ACTUALLY DID at their meeting:

  • Affordable Housing Shift:

    Adopted an ordinance strengthening requirements for on-site affordable housing in certain Citywide Density Bonus projects, especially near transit, meaning fewer projects can opt to pay a fee instead of building affordable units.

  • Lake Austin Drawdown Protest:

    Heard significant public opposition from local business owners and residents regarding the planned October-December Lake Austin drawdown, citing major economic losses and urging a delay to winter months.

  • Workforce & Public Safety Funding:

    Committed an additional $2.55 million to extend the Austin Infrastructure Academy for local workforce training and renewed a $19.5 million contract for emergency medical services with Travis County.

  • Major Zoning Decisions Delayed:

    Postponed votes on several significant zoning requests, including a conditional use permit appeal for the Girls' School of Austin and large multi-family proposals in areas like Circle C and West 49th Street.

  • Community & Event Boosts:

    Approved early preparations for the 2027 South by Southwest festivals and established a new advisory board to support the Millennium Youth Entertainment Complex, a vital East Austin community hub.
