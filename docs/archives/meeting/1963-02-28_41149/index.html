Austin's Future: Hospitals, Abattoir, Zoning, Cable

Thursday, February 28, 1963 regular

Here are the key takeaways from the February 28, 1963 Austin City Council agenda:

  • Future of Healthcare Planning:

    The City Council discussed funding a $35,000 study by a major consultant to plan Austin's long-term health and hospital needs, particularly for city-owned Brackenridge Hospital, emphasizing inter-hospital cooperation.

  • Managing City Businesses:

    A significant policy decision was made to lease the city's Abattoir (meat processing plant) to a local group, aiming to eliminate ongoing financial deficits without selling the facility.

  • Development Debates and Public Input:

    While several land rezoning requests were approved for growth, proposals for a motor company expansion and a nursery were denied or postponed due to strong neighborhood opposition and concerns over "spot zoning," leading to council site visits.

  • Embracing New Technology:

    The Council authorized negotiations to lease city properties, including electric substation sites, to cable TV companies, addressing the infrastructure needs for this emerging service.
