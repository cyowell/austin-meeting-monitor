Austin Plans Future: Zoning, Bonds, Freedom Train

Thursday, February 5, 1948 regular

  • Major City Growth and Infrastructure:

    Approved substantial new water and sewer infrastructure, along with permits for extensive gas line installations. Plans were also finalized to sell $3.12 million in bonds to fund future projects, including schools, parks, and electric and water plant upgrades.

  • Zoning Changes Reshape Neighborhoods:

    Multiple zoning reclassifications passed, allowing for new apartment complexes and commercial developments in areas previously designated residential. However, one commercial rezone was rejected by the Board of Adjustment to preserve a residential block.

  • Welcoming the Freedom Train:

    The city appropriated $3,000 to host the national "Freedom Train," ensuring free access for all citizens and prohibiting additional public contributions.

  • Boom in Taxi Services:

    Numerous new licenses were granted for taxi operators and drivers, including probationary permits for some who faced initial denial, signaling a rapidly expanding local transportation sector. Notably, one of the new taxi operators was identified as "Colored," reflecting the societal norms of the era.
