Austin: Parking Meter Debate, Zoning Expansions

Thursday, April 16, 1936 regular

Here's a summary of the Austin City Council meeting from April 16, 1936:

  • Parking Meter Delay:

    The city decided against immediate installation of parking meters, instead agreeing to postpone implementation until January 1937, allowing local merchants to run an educational campaign for better parking.

  • Major Zoning Shifts:

    Public hearings were set for significant zoning changes on South Congress Avenue and Oak Hill Road, proposing to reclassify large residential areas into commercial districts. The council also approved immediate residential-to-commercial rezoning for properties along Lydia Street.

  • Utility Bond Refinancing:

    The city approved a plan to cancel $404,000 in existing utility bonds (Water, Electric Light, and Sewer Systems) that carried 4% interest, and re-issue new bonds at a lower 3% interest rate to fund system repairs and extensions.

  • Street Name Standardized:

    A section of Waverly Avenue was officially renamed Harris Boulevard to eliminate confusion, as it served as a direct extension of the existing Harris Boulevard.
