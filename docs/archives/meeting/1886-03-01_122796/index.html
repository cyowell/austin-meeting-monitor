Austin 1886: Tax, Infrastructure & Public Order

Monday, March 1, 1886 regular

• Passed multiple public safety ordinances regulating police interference, disturbances of peace, animal control, and public decency—establishing Austin's core crime and public welfare rules.

• Approved city street improvements, including flagstone crossings on Congress Avenue and Pecan Street, a potential bridge on College Avenue, and regulations for railroad track maintenance.

• Levied municipal taxes at 1% for general city operations and an additional 0.25% specifically for public schools, generating revenue for Austin's growth.

• Implemented transportation regulations designating official taxi/hack stands on city streets and requiring lit lamps on licensed vehicles for public safety.

• Addressed health and sanitation through ordinances against diseased animals, dead animal removal, and food safety to protect residents from disease.
