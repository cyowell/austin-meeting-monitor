Austin's Future: Energy, Downtown, and Local Battles

Thursday, October 5, 1978 regular

  • Landmark Nuclear Fuel Settlement:

    The city announced a substantial settlement with Westinghouse for the South Texas Project's nuclear fuel, valued over $300 million. This deal is expected to significantly reduce future fuel costs and lower the city's nuclear fuel inventory requirements by $10-15 million.

  • Downtown Parking Policy Overhaul:

    New ordinances passed to stimulate downtown revitalization by easing off-street parking requirements in the Central Business District. This allows for contributions to a new parking fund as an alternative, with vigorous debate heard from property owners and city commissions.

  • Contentious Street Closure Debate:

    A heated public hearing took place regarding the vacation of a portion of Nasco Drive, pitting local residents against the Allandale Baptist Church over concerns about traffic, safety, and neighborhood character, with the council ultimately voting to proceed with the closure.

  • City Employee Raises & New Energy Initiatives:

    Pay plans for city employees were revised and approved. Additionally, the council endorsed and funded new energy conservation efforts, including an "On-Site Energy Audit Program" for low-income homes and "Project Benchmark" to track and advise on household energy use.

  • Debates Over Social Services Funding:

    Discussions highlighted challenges in providing city funds for administrative costs of social service programs like "Aid to Victims in Distress," with councilmembers debating the legality of using "emergency" budget amendments.
