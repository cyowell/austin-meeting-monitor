Austin Paves Streets, Adjusts Taxes, & Early Auto Perks

Thursday, May 28, 1914 regular

  • Massive Street Improvement Projects:

    The city initiated extensive grading, paving, and guttering for numerous streets, including West 19th, 9th, 6th, Colorado, Guadalupe, and Brazos. Property owners were assessed costs, and legal action was directed against those who refused to agree to payment.

  • Widespread Tax Assessment Corrections:

    Multiple residents received adjustments or outright cancellations for their 1912-1913 tax assessments, primarily concerning "money on hand" or "credits," often due to documented errors.

  • Glimpses into Early Automobile Life:

    The Council remitted a speeding fine for an automobile driver and granted a permit for a "Quick Service Fire Repair Company" to install a "free air" pipe for cars under a sidewalk.

  • Unique Public & Private Matters:

    The city issued duplicate cemetery deeds, authorized payment for a lost employee warrant, and notably, credited the Austin Statesman newspaper for employee wages lost during a severe storm that caused a city power outage.
