ATX No Wrong Door: Debate & Big Investments

Here's what happened at the Austin City Work Session:

  • "No Wrong Door" Program Update:

    City leaders received an update on the "No Wrong Door" initiative, a multi-agency program designed to coordinate crisis care for individuals with complex mental health, substance use, and housing challenges who frequently interact with emergency and justice systems.

  • Civil Liberties & Privacy Debate:

    The program sparked significant public debate. Numerous speakers, including mental health advocates, public defenders, and medical professionals, raised serious concerns about civil liberties, patient privacy, and due process, particularly regarding law enforcement involvement and data sharing without legal counsel present.

  • Preliminary Positive Impact:

    Supporters and staff presented preliminary data suggesting the "No Wrong Door" program is reducing police and EMS contacts for high utilizers (e.g., a 31% overall reduction in police contacts for the top 99 participants) and increasing engagement with social services. Roughly 40% of these targeted individuals have voluntarily signed consent agreements.

  • Major Mental Health Infrastructure Investments:

    Travis County and Central Health outlined substantial new investments in mental health care. These include $91 million for a future Mental Health Crisis Center, $33.1 million for new psychiatric emergency department bays and inpatient beds, and $16 million committed to establishing a comprehensive crisis center with city and county partners.

  • Future Crisis Care Vision:

    A long-term, multi-phase plan was detailed for a comprehensive mental health crisis care system for Austin and Travis County, projecting significant expansion of psychiatric emergency, inpatient, sobering, detox, and crisis residential facilities to meet community needs.
