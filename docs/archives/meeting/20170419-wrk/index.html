Austin's Fiscal Future: Growth Slows, Tax Cap Looms

Wednesday, April 19, 2017 Austin City Council Budget Work Session
  • Austin's economic boom is moderating, with job and sales tax growth slowing from previous highs, while housing costs and mobility issues continue to challenge the middle class.
  • The city's general fund heavily relies on property taxes (43%), which are projected to see strong growth to compensate for slower increases in sales tax, utility transfers, and other revenue streams.
  • Over two-thirds of the city's general fund budget is dedicated to public safety, with 72% of overall expenditures going towards personnel costs, making significant budget cuts challenging without impacting services.
  • Upcoming state legislation proposing a 5% cap on property tax revenue growth (down from 8%) presents a significant long-term financial threat, potentially cutting tens of millions from the city's budget by FY22.
  • A median Austin homeowner is projected to see a roughly $5.20 monthly increase in their city property tax bill under the existing 8% roll-back rate for the upcoming fiscal year.
