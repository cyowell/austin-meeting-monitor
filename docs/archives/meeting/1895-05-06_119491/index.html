Austin's 1895 Cotton Mill & Water System

Monday, May 6, 1895 regular

• Major investment opportunity: City Council debated a major proposition from George N. Todd to build a large cotton factory in Austin. The project promised significant growth, including construction within 18 months with 15,000 spindles initially and expansion to 30,000 spindles over five years. The city would provide free water/power for 25 years.

• Water and electric systems launching: The city's new water and electric light systems were operational, with over 500 water customers and 106 electric light applications already filed. The council approved contracts to supply water and electricity to the Deaf and Dumb Asylum and the International & Great Northern Railroad.

• Debt management and financial restructuring: The council approved a complex series of bond exchanges designed to reduce the city's $1.4 million waterworks debt by $50,000, with the Finance Committee recommending strategic purchases of lower-cost bonds.

• Infrastructure and safety regulations: The council passed fire safety ordinance requirements for escape routes at public gathering places and authorized citizens to privately construct water line extensions at their own expense, expanding service to underserved areas.

• Public services expansion: Monthly reports showed the city providing hospitalization, cemetery services, fire protection, and police services. Officers also managed water distribution challenges as demand grew faster than infrastructure could reach outlying areas.

It will hardly be contended that the enforcement of an ordinance, having for its purpose the protection of life and limb, will work a great hardship and serious injury to persons owning or leasing buildings, room or part of a room wherein any hall or other public amusement is given or held, nor do we believe it will be contended that places of worship, such as the First Baptist church, the Tenth street Methodist church and the Christian church can be properly termed 'places of public amusement' within the meaning of the ordinance. Your committee finds that a building affected by the provisions of the ordinance, having a number of windows, may be furnished with the necessary fire escapes at a trifling cost, compared with the loss of life and injury that might result in case of fire with no means of escape. In case of the Board of Trade building, the opera house and the Young Men's Christian Association hall, two or more fire escapes, to the ground, properly connected with all the windows by an iron balcony, would conform to the requirements of the ordinance. We therefore return the petition with the recommendation that it be not granted and further, that section 3 be so amended as to hereafter read as follows: "Section 3. That any person or persons violating any of the provisions of sections 1, 2, and 5A of this ordinance shall on conviction, be fined not less than twenty-five dollars nor more than one hundred dollars."
