Christian, F. A. Scholz and P. H. Todd. His Hon. J. B. Wheeler stated that the Council had come together for the purpose of receiving his veto Message upon the resolution passed June 1st 1874 requiring him to contract with Sylvester Watts for twenty gas lamp posts, and gas for the city, for twenty lamps at the rate of six dollars per one thousand cubic feet. Whereupon he read his veto Message as follows, Mayors Office Austin Tex June 2nd 1874 To the members of the City Council of Austin, Texas Gentlemen; I cannot conscientiously sign this resolution which requires the city to close contract with Sylvester Watts for 20 lamp posts, lamps, and fixtures at $3.50 each. This resolution, if enforced, will cost the city at once $1000, cash which to say the least of it, will very much embarrass our financial arrangement. Besides I am fully convinced that if we were to advertise for bids for doing said work that the City would save several hundred dollars, and perhaps get better material and work. I am also op- posed to the other portion of the resolution which compels the city to take gas at $6.00 per 1000 feet. That price is certainly extravagant, it will cost the city near $200.00 each month to light the 20 lamps. --- Page break --- 476 and those lamps too are to be located on only two streets and that too in a very small portion of those streets. (We are now lighting the streets about the same distance and our oil for the last month has cost us just $300 currency. The oil lamps are now erected, and can be used by the city without further cost to the city – If necessary we can estab-lish say 10 or 12 more of these lamps at about $10.00 each, which will do to use until the Gas Com-pany can and will erect lamps and let us have gas at reasonable rates. By adopting this course now and lighting economically for a while we can repair our streets, place every thing on a cash basis and very soon we can be in a posi-tion that we will have funds to do all necessa- -ry repairs – But to follow up these resolutions at this time, it will cripple us financially and and very much interfere with the desired improve-ments of our streets and bridges; besides it will each month be very difficult to pay the $200.00 dol-lars cash and pay all other necessary expenses of the city. I do Trust and hope that you will figure carefully and consider well, before you fos-ter on the city in our present condition, this very extravagant luxury, to the great detriment of may other very necessary improvements. Hoping that you will give these hasty written objections due consideration. I remain yours respectfully J. B. Whether Mayor. Ald.
