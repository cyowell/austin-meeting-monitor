August 12, 2026
Wednesday
Regular Meeting
Completed

What the Council Did

  • Approved the City Budget and Utility Rate Increases: The Council officially adopted the Fiscal Year 2026-2027 budget, setting the property tax rate and approving rate increases for city utilities like Austin Energy, Austin Water, Austin Resource Recovery (trash/recycling), and Watershed Protection (drainage). Many residents spoke during public hearings to oppose these utility rate hikes, citing affordability concerns.
  • Addressed Social Services Funding and Oversight: After significant public input regarding cuts, the budget was approved with amendments impacting social services. The Council also passed resolutions to develop a process for reviewing social service contract performance and to evaluate specific programs proposed for reductions or elimination in the budget.
  • Moved Towards Independent 911 Communications: In a notable decision, the Council approved a resolution to establish an Office of Joint Emergency Communications, with the clear directive to work towards creating an independent 911 department separate from the Austin Police Department. This aims to better match emergency responses to the nature of the crisis.
  • Increased Police Transparency and Accountability: The Council passed resolutions directing the City Manager to review key performance measures for the Austin Police Department. They also called for a summary list of police misconduct complaints (known as the "G-file") for Council review and an assessment of creating a publicly accessible online database for such allegations.
  • Supported Housing Programs and Microbusinesses: Over $90 million was approved for various housing programs to be managed by the Austin Housing Finance Corporation, aimed at supporting housing affordability. Additionally, a resolution passed to propose City Code changes to define "microbusinesses," reduce their permitting and licensing requirements, and develop a program to waive City fees for qualifying small enterprises.

What the Council Did

Here's a summary of what the Austin Housing Finance Corporation (AHFC) Board of Directors did at their August 12, 2026 meeting:

  • Approved Major Operating Budget: The Board adopted a grant operating budget of nearly $42 million for AHFC and an additional $1.7 million for the Housing Assistance Fund for the upcoming fiscal year (FY 26-27). This money will fund the day-to-day operations and various housing programs.
  • Approved Capital Budget for Housing Projects: A separate capital budget of over $48.5 million was approved for FY 26-27. This funding is dedicated to significant housing development projects.
  • Authorized Management of City Housing Programs: The Board approved an agreement for AHFC to manage and operate various housing programs on behalf of the City of Austin for FY 26-27, with a budget not to exceed $90.5 million. This ensures the continued operation of important housing services for residents.
  • All Items Passed Unanimously: All three items were approved together as part of a "consent agenda" without any public speakers, discussion, or objection from the board members, indicating quick and unanimous support.

What the Council Did

  • Set November Elections: The Council approved ordinances to hold three separate elections on November 3, 2026. This includes a general election for City Council members in Districts 1, 3, 5, 8, and 9; a bond election for proposed Parks, Recreation, and Library improvements; and a special election for proposed city charter amendments, including one initiated by citizens.
  • Debated the City Budget: Council held a detailed presentation and public discussion of the City Manager's proposed budget for Fiscal Year 2026-2027. This was a discussion session, with final votes on the budget scheduled for later in August.
  • Challenged Police Funding: Many residents spoke against the proposed $25 million increase for the Austin Police Department (APD) budget. Speakers cited concerns about police accountability, recent incidents, and argued the funds should be redirected.
  • Advocated for Social Services: Community members strongly urged the Council to fully fund social services, including programs for unhoused residents, mental health support, food access, senior meals, and workforce development, expressing concern over proposed cuts in these areas.
  • Pushed for Independent 911: There was significant public input supporting the creation of an independent department for the 911 call center, moving it out from under APD.
  • Criticized Homeless Encampment Sweeps: Many speakers called for a halt or pause to the city's policy of sweeping homeless encampments, highlighting the negative impact on individuals and advocating for more investment in safe storage and housing solutions.
